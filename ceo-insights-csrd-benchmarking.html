CEO Brief

CSRD Benchmarking: Danish Global Brands

Executive Headline
Ten Danish global companies published their first mandatory CSRD sustainability statements in 2025. The average composite score was 4.0 out of 5.0 across three lenses. The 1.6-point spread between best and worst is not a reporting gap. It is a strategy gap - and it is already visible to investors.
The Shift - What Is Changing

CSRD has moved ESG disclosure from voluntary to mandatory, from narrative to assured, and from communications to governance. Wave 1 reports are now public documents that investors, lenders, and proxy advisors are actively benchmarking. The companies that treated CSRD preparation as a reporting exercise look different in the data from those that used it to stress-test their strategy.

Why It Matters - Business Impact
📊
The Integration Gap
Only 4 of 10 companies have ESG genuinely embedded in capital allocation. Ørsted, Maersk, Vestas, and Danfoss use shadow carbon pricing or SBTi-linked metrics in investment decisions. The other 6 report ESG but do not yet use it to decide.
⚠️
Scenario Analysis Gap
Only 4 companies conducted scenario analysis with named reference scenarios and quantified financial outcomes. This is the most common gap in Wave 1 and the hardest to close under time pressure.
💎
The Pandora Signal
The peer group's most significant surprise. 100% recycled metals achieved. Only company with reasonable assurance on selected metrics. TNFD referenced. A company not typically seen as an ESG leader making one of the strongest cases in the group.

The companies at the top of this ranking did not score well because CSRD required it. They scored well because they had already decided that integrating sustainability into financial strategy was worth doing. The report reflects the practice - not the other way around.

Section 2 What the Scorecard Shows
Only 4 of 10 companies - Ørsted, Novo Nordisk, Danfoss, and Danske Bank - conducted scenario analysis with named reference scenarios (IEA, IPCC, NGFS) and quantified financial outcomes. Bang and Olufsen explicitly stated no precise climate scenarios had been defined.
Only 4 companies have genuinely integrated ESG into financial decision-making: Ørsted (taxonomy-aligned investment), Maersk (USD 75/t shadow carbon price in all investment decisions), Vestas (SBTi-linked per-MWh intensity targets), and Danfoss (internal carbon price in CapEx, sustainability-linked bond of EUR 500m).
Pandora is the peer group's most significant surprise: only company to obtain reasonable assurance (not just limited) on selected metrics; 100% recycled silver and gold achieved 2025; TNFD referenced; strongest responsible sourcing programme in the group.
Nature and biodiversity is the most uneven parameter in the scorecard - ranging from 5 (Ørsted: net-positive biodiversity target from 2030) to 1 (Bang and Olufsen: E4 assessed as out of scope). This is the parameter most likely to require remediation as ESRS E4 scrutiny increases.
The most credible reports explicitly disclose gaps - Maersk footnotes unassured data points, FLSmidth explains every restatement, Carlsberg discloses its SBTi target deferral to Q1 2026. Big 4 benchmarking consistently finds that transparent disclosure of limitations correlates with higher underlying data quality.
Danfoss, a private company, achieved a 3.9 composite score - equivalent to listed-company standard. It reduced its own emissions by 33% in a single year and embedded an internal carbon price into CapEx decisions. Private company status is no longer a reason for lower disclosure quality.
Section 3 What Is Coming Next
HorizonWhat changes and what triggers it
NowWave 1 CSRD reports are public. Institutional investors, proxy advisors, and lenders are actively benchmarking them. Scenario analysis gaps, missing transition plan detail, and weak financial connectivity are now visible in assured, mandatory documents - not voluntary reports.
12 monthsThe European Commission's Omnibus simplification proposals will reduce mandatory data points for Wave 2 and 3 reporters. Companies using this as a reason to slow down will discover that investor and lender scrutiny does not simplify in parallel with regulation. The companies that prepared to the higher standard will be comparatively better positioned.
3 to 5 yearsESG disclosure quality becomes a standard input into credit assessment, equity valuation, and M&A due diligence. The 1.6-point spread visible in this peer group today translates into a measurable cost-of-capital differential. Companies that have not closed the integration gap by then will be explaining it to investors and boards under time pressure.
Section 4 What This Means for the Business
Scenario analysis
The most common gap in Wave 1 and the hardest to close quickly. Companies that begin scenario development now - using named reference scenarios (IEA NZE, IPCC RCP 2.6/8.5, NGFS) and quantifying financial outcomes - will be materially better positioned for Wave 2 assurance.
Capital allocation
The companies at the top of this ranking use an internal carbon price or SBTi-linked metric in investment decisions. The reporting reflects the practice. Companies that price carbon internally before they are required to report it produce measurably stronger disclosures - and make better long-term investment decisions.
Assurance readiness
Retrospective data assembly is the primary root cause of assurance control gaps. Companies need named ESG data owners, documented calculation methodologies, and audit-trail capable systems before the assurance process begins - not during it.
Nature and biodiversity
ESRS E4 is the parameter with the widest spread in this peer group. Companies that have assessed nature as immaterial need to be confident that assessment will withstand scrutiny as TNFD adoption increases and investor attention to nature-related financial risks grows.
Transparency as strategy
Disclosing gaps is less damaging than obscuring them. Maersk, FLSmidth, Carlsberg, and Bang and Olufsen all score higher on transparency than their overall composite would suggest - because they explicitly disclosed what was missing. That approach is correlated with higher underlying data quality in every Big 4 benchmarking framework.
Cost of inaction
Companies that use the Omnibus simplification as a reason to reduce CSRD investment will find themselves explaining the gap to investors and lenders who are already using Wave 1 disclosures as a baseline. The compliance window narrows, but the scrutiny does not.
